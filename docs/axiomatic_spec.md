## 1. The problem in one sentence

Given only information available at month-end \(t\), rank 50–100 US-listed ETFs by their next-month excess return, and use the model's **epistemic uncertainty** to build a long-only mean-CVaR portfolio whose tail risk is no worse than point-estimate DRO and mean-variance, net of costs.

This is a **methods study**. It is not a stock-selection model, a market-timing model, a causal model, or a live strategy.

---

## 2. Data-generating mechanism

An ETF's monthly total return is generated by four layers:

1. **Factor exposure.** The ETF's holdings load on market, size, value, momentum, and sector/industry factors. Most of the cross-sectional variance is compensation for these exposures, not alpha.
2. **Idiosyncratic holdings noise.** Tracking error, rebalancing, and constituent-specific events produce asset-specific noise.
3. **Flows and arbitrage.** Creation/redemption activity and primary-market arbitrage can move prices away from NAV; the effect is usually small for liquid ETFs but is part of the mechanism.
4. **Metadata and selection.** Expense ratio, distribution yield, age, and liquidity affect investor demand and the probability that an ETF survives. These are **selection variables**, not causal drivers of next-month returns.

The target is next-month **excess** return:

\[
r^{\text{ex}}_{i,t+1} = r_{i,t+1} - r_{f,t+1}
\]

where \(r_{i,t+1}\) is the total return implied by distribution-adjusted prices and \(r_{f,t+1}\) is the monthly risk-free return converted from FRED `TB3MS`.

**Timing:** decision at month-end \(t\); outcome over \((t, t+1]\). No feature may use information after \(t\).

## 3. What is predictable, and what is not

**Potentially predictable (small):**

- cross-sectional momentum and short-term reversal;
- volatility and drawdown state;
- liquidity and dollar-volume conditions;
- factor exposures and beta;
- dispersion/regime interactions.

**Not predictable by this design:**

- the direction of the aggregate market;
- macro shocks;
- ETF closures;
- the exact timing of flows;
- anything requiring information after month-end \(t\).

**What the model is not allowed to use:**

- future returns, future constituents, future closures;
- revised data that was not available at \(t\);
- current metadata presented as if it were historical;
- the target in any feature;
- test-period information in tuning or feature selection.

## 4. Economic and institutional constraints

| Constraint | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Long-only, \(w_i \ge 0\) | No shorting a rich ETF or hedging with negative weights. |
| \(\sum_i w_i = 1\) | Fully invested; no leverage and no cash timing. |
| \(w_i \le 0.05\) | At least 20 holdings; limits concentration in a lucky ETF. |
| Monthly rebalance | Turnover and cost matter; no intraday alpha. |
| 10 bps one-way cost (primary) | A high-turnover signal can die after costs. |
| 50–100 ETF universe | Lower breadth and dispersion than a stock universe; power is limited. |
| Public ETF data | Closed funds may be missing; results are not survivorship-free. |

These constraints are part of the problem, not implementation details. A model that only works without them is not evidence for this project.

## 5. Bayes risk / noise floor (G-R1 completion)

The theoretical maximum \(R^2\) for next-month cross-sectional returns is:

\[
R^2_{\max} = \frac{\operatorname{Var}_i\left(\mathbb{E}_t[r^{\text{ex}}_{i,t+1}]\right)}
{\operatorname{Var}_i\left(r^{\text{ex}}_{i,t+1}\right)}
\]

The irreducible noise is the residual variance after the true conditional mean is removed. In monthly ETF returns, the denominator is dominated by factor and idiosyncratic noise, so \(R^2_{\max}\) is expected to be **low single-digit percent at best**. The exact estimate is a G1 deliverable:

- [ ] Compute the cross-sectional variance of next-month excess returns for every month.
- [ ] Estimate the factor-explained share with a rolling factor regression.
- [ ] Estimate the residual (irreducible) variance.
- [ ] Record the implied \(R^2_{\max}\) and the corresponding expected Sharpe improvement.
- [ ] Date and sign the estimate **before** the first model fit.

**Rule:** if the estimated \(R^2_{\max}\) is indistinguishable from zero, the ML layer cannot add value. The honest response is to report that and stop — not to add capacity until something appears.

**No numeric claim is made here until the G1 snapshot exists.** A placeholder number would violate the no-fabrication rule.

## 6. Why an uncertainty-aware DRO formulation is compatible with the mechanism

- The forecast is noisy and heteroskedastic; a point estimate hides that.
- The box ambiguity set \(|\mu_i - \hat{\mu}_i| \le \kappa \hat{\sigma}_i\) maps epistemic uncertainty directly into the worst-case mean.
- CVaR controls the tail that a variance-only objective misses.
- The long-only, capped, fully-invested constraints keep the problem convex and auditable.
- A solver failure is fatal, so the reported weights are always the optimizer's output.

This is a risk-management mechanism, not an alpha claim.

## 7. Falsifiable predictions

| Prediction | Test | What would falsify it |
|---|---|---|
| The uncertainty layer reduces tail risk | M1 vs B5 on max drawdown and CVaR95, net of costs | No improvement, or improvement within noise |
| The method is competitive on net Sharpe | M1 vs best baseline, 0.20 non-inferiority margin | Net Sharpe worse by more than 0.20 |
| The mechanism is the uncertainty layer | A2 ablation | No-uncertainty variant matches or beats M1 |
| The result is not cost-driven | 5/10/20/50 bps sensitivity | Sign flips at plausible costs |
| The result is not a data artifact | Target-shuffling, PIT audit, universe audit | Any leakage test fails |
| The model beats a simple forecast | Ridge B0 vs LightGBM, Diebold-Mariano | LightGBM fails to beat Ridge at \(p < 0.01\) |
